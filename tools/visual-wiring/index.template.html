Each of the five signals needs a 2.2 kΩ pull-up to board 3.3 V and a 100 nF capacitor to board GND. The five are PB1 and PG12–PG15. Leave the STOP +5 V pins empty and remove STOP4–7 DIAG jumper caps.

Parts, supply adjustment and checks before this circuit can be accepted

The new alarm supply is a Mean Well DDR-15G-24, fed from the existing protected 24 V control supply. Set its isolated output to 23.0 V before attaching drives; verify it stays at or below 24.0 V under no load and all four relay loads. Input protection is a separate selection; the 0.25 A output fuse is not its input fuse.

This circuit requires ALM conducting when healthy, opening on alarm and drive-power loss. The exact installed drive setting and unpowered state still need proving. Save the drive parameters, test each alarm, and test open ALM, open COM− and lost alarm supply. Do not use NC contacts to disguise incorrect alarm polarity.

The relay outputs are ordinary contacts. Stuck contacts or an ALM-to-COM− short can mask a fault. This circuit is not a certified emergency-stop function. All installation and acceptance checks remain open.

Firmware: PB1 remains the fault-stop path. The new diagnostic candidate reports raw MR1F:1,mask snapshots for PG12–PG15; these do not stop motion or latch the first fault. Older or unidentified installed firmware must show the axis state as unknown until its reporting capability is verified.

Eight PUL/DIR inputs need up to 128 mA. Select and terminate the source connection and wire for the fused branch, then verify loaded voltage, current, pulse timing and reset behavior with drive power off. The fuse provides overcurrent protection, not overvoltage protection or isolation.
